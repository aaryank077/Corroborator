# Reconstruction: Haneda runway collision, 2 January 2024

Working file for the Corroborate case study. Draft by Claude from archived pages, with editorial calls by the author marked **CALL**.

## The event (end state, for reference)

At 17:47 JST (08:47 UTC) Japan Airlines Flight 516, an Airbus A350, collided on landing at Haneda with a Japan Coast Guard Dash 8 that was on the runway without clearance. All 379 people on the JAL plane (367 passengers, 12 crew) got out. Five of six Coast Guard crew died; the captain survived, seriously injured. Final injury count on the JAL plane: 17. Investigators (JTSB) later found the evacuation finished at 17:58, 11 minutes after the collision, not 18:05 as JAL first said. Source for the end state: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Haneda_Airport_runway_collision), which cites the JTSB reports. **To do:** cite the JTSB progress report (25 Dec 2024) directly.

All times are UTC. **T+** = time since the collision. Add 9 hours for Japan time.

### Item 7: JAL's full answer (NHK live text)

> 「滑走路上という認識で今現在ではいる。ただ着陸許可が出ていたかや管制との通信については事故原因の根幹に関わることなので、今のところ申し上げられず、確認中だ」…「通常であれば着陸許可が出ていたと考えるが、原因追及は運輸安全委員会で客観的にしていただく必要があり、ここで断定的な話ができないことをご容赦いただければ」
> — Noriyuki Aoki, JAL managing executive officer, as reported by NHK

Translation: "Our current understanding is that it was on the runway. However, whether landing permission had been given, and the communication with air traffic control, go to the heart of the cause, so for now we can't say; we're confirming it." … "Normally, we would assume landing permission had been given, but the cause needs to be established objectively by the Japan Transport Safety Board, so please forgive us for not being able to speak definitively here."

- CNN quoted the first sentence: "I can't say at this time if they were cleared to land."
- AP turned the second into "the airline's understanding is that the JAL flight had received permission to land," dropping the hedge.
- Kyodo's wording, 「着陸許可が出ていたと認識している」, doesn't match NHK's 「通常であれば…と考える」 ("normally we would assume"). It may be a paraphrase or a different moment in the same press conference.

### Item 8: was it known the plane had no permission?

**When the remark was made: no.** At that same briefing, the ministry explicitly declined to say whether the Coast Guard plane had clearance. "Preparing to take off" described what the plane appeared to be doing, while the clearance question was openly unanswered.

**But within about 6½ hours, yes.** NHK reported at T+10:47 (19:34 UTC, 04:34 JST on 3 January), citing ministry officials, that the plane had been told to stop short of the runway. AP's story, as archived at T+12:31 and again at T+17:26, still carried "preparing to take off" with no mention of that.

**CALL:** *fair when said; fine.* It accurately reported what officials described while clearance was openly unconfirmed. Not counted as an error.

## What this shows

1. **Three outlets, one source.** Every casualty figure in CNN's first hour of crash coverage cited NHK, and AP's first version leaned on NHK too. → *Supports counting independent sources, not reports.*
2. **Holding language until confirmation works.** Both English outlets kept the Coast Guard crew "missing" until there was official confirmation. → *Supports "say what's missing."*
3. **Roundups strip attribution.** CNN's roundup merged NHK and JAL injury figures into one unattributed sentence. → *Design: summaries keep each fact's source.*
4. **Numbers drift, and downstream copies freeze them.** NHK moved its injury count 1 → 17 → 14. CNN kept 17 and AP used 14, so each outlet froze a different moment of the same source. The passenger count was also wrong on one CNN post. → *Design: flag the same quantity with different values; and when a root source revises a figure, flag every line that depends on it.*
5. **"Confirmed" isn't permanent.** JAL's own evacuation time was later replaced by the investigators'. → *Design: the "superseded" state, with "as of when, from whom."*
6. **Each outlet quoted half an answer.** JAL's answer on clearance contained both a refusal and an assumption. CNN kept one half, AP the other. → *Design: store the exact words; this is the strongest evidence for it.*
7. **Translation shifts meaning.** 重傷 ("seriously injured") became "critical condition." 消防車両 ("fire-service vehicles") became "fire trucks." → **New design need:** when a source is in another language, keep the original wording next to the translation.

## Author's calls

1. **Captain's condition:** *acceptable imprecision.* Confirmed after checking the Japanese. NHK said 重傷 ("seriously injured") throughout and CNN rendered it as "critical condition." That's a shift in severity, but it doesn't claim a death and correctly reports a serious injury. It still supports keeping the original wording beside a translation.
2. **Fire vehicles:** acceptable as long as it's attributed to NHK. Noted that NHK itself first said 70+ and that its term includes all fire-service vehicles.
3. **JAL press conference:** resolved with NHK's full text (above).
4. **"Preparing to take off":** *fair when said; fine.* Clearance was openly unconfirmed at the time, and the phrase described what officials reported.
5. **NHK archive:** checked (this version).
